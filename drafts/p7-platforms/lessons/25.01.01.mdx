# OLTP vs OLAP

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A transactional app runs expensive monthly reports.

## Step 1 — Mental model

OLTP optimizes short operational transactions; OLAP scans and aggregates analytical data. Separate workloads when measurements justify it.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Compare point lookup with a synthetic event aggregation and outline an isolated reporting path.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Operational queries stay bounded and analytics has defined freshness.

**Negative:** Heavy reporting starving production writes fails.

## Qualification boundary

In-memory count/deduplication model; no warehouse, materialized-view freshness, ClickHouse or dbt runtime qualification.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

An operational point lookup and a reporting aggregation have different access patterns. OLTP typically favors short indexed transactions, while analytics may scan many rows and compute grouped results. Separation is justified by observed contention, freshness requirements and cost, not simply because a warehouse is fashionable.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Use a synthetic order dataset and compare a single-order lookup against a monthly aggregation. Record query plan, elapsed time and concurrent write latency; do not infer a workload capacity from one run.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Propose a reporting path with an explicit data grain, freshness target and isolation strategy. Compare read replica, materialized view and separate warehouse under the same requirements.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual database, analytics tools and reviewer evidence.
