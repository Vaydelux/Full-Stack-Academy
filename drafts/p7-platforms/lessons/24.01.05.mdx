# iOS signing basics

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

An iOS app needs to be installed on a test device.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Signing ties an app identity to an authorized provisioning path; simulator success is not a signed-device result.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

List bundle ID, entitlement and provisioning requirements; use a permitted development account for device testing.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** A signed development build is attributable to the correct identity.

**Negative:** Sharing signing private keys or claiming simulator success proves device signing fails.

Inspect the complementary `bridgeAllowed` teaching model. Extend its assertions when relevant; it is not an implementation of the full external technology.

node --test --test-name-pattern="24[.]01" examples/p7-platforms/labs.test.mjs

## Qualification boundary

Native builds, WebView isolation and plugin permissions require a real device.

## Knowledge check

1. What is the invariant and trust boundary?
2. Which negative result falsifies an unsafe design?
3. Which real tool or reviewer must supply missing evidence?

**Assessment:** Record exact fixture input, expected and actual output, negative evidence and not-run checks. Do not substitute a design or model for a real acceptance result.
