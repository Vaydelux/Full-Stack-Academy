# WebRTC and LiveKit

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

Two browsers need a live voice connection.

## Step 1 — Mental model

WebRTC media transport depends on signaling, ICE/STUN/TURN, permissions and network conditions; SFU is a separate component.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

In isolated browser test two synthetic peers and authorized room tokens; exercise blocked permission and reconnect.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Authorized peers connect and denied participant cannot join.

**Negative:** Assuming direct connectivity always works or public room access fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

WebRTC coordinates media transport using signaling and connectivity establishment; STUN helps discover connectivity and TURN can relay traffic when direct connectivity is unavailable. An SFU is a separate topology choice. Room authorization and device permission remain application responsibilities.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Use two authorized local peers with synthetic media, then deny a device permission and interrupt connectivity. Inspect negotiation and recovery state; confirm unauthorized room entry is rejected independently of media negotiation.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Record connectivity path, setup latency, reconnect and device permission behavior. Test relay-dependent cases only with permitted infrastructure and document unavailable conditions rather than inferring Internet-wide reliability.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual media tools, storage, playback, provider and reviewer evidence.
