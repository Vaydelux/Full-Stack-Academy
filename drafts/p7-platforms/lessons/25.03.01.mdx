# PostGIS geometry and geography

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A search endpoint stores point locations.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Geometry uses a coordinate reference system; geography models geodetic distances. Document SRID and longitude/latitude order.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Create disposable PostGIS points with SRID 4326; compare distance behavior for geometry/geography.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Coordinates and units match documented query.

**Negative:** Mixing degrees and meters without conversion fails.

## Qualification boundary

Coordinate validation model only; real PostGIS geometry, index, map rendering and location privacy require separate acceptance.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A spatial record must declare a coordinate reference system and coordinate ordering. Longitude and latitude in geographic coordinates are not planar distances in meters. Choose geometry or geography based on the required predicates, coordinate system and extent; document units in every distance contract.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Create a disposable PostGIS fixture containing two known synthetic points with SRID 4326. Compare geometry distance in coordinate units with geography distance in meters; reject mixed SRIDs unless deliberately transformed.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Write a schema and API contract specifying coordinate order, SRID, distance units and validation. Record SQL, result units and expected approximate distance rather than interpreting an arbitrary numeric result as meters.
