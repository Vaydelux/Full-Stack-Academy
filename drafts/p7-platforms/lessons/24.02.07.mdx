# Reconnect and retry

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A connection repeatedly drops during synchronization.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Bound retries, use backoff and stable IDs, and preserve acknowledgements across reconnects.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Simulate request timeout after server accepts an operation; replay same ID and reconcile acknowledgement.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** One logical operation is applied.

**Negative:** Creating a new ID on retry causes duplicate work.

## Qualification boundary

In-memory sync model; actual SQLite persistence, durable queue, crash recovery and conflict reconciliation are not established.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A timeout is uncertain acknowledgement, not evidence of rejection. Persist the original operation and replay the same identifier under a bounded retry budget. Permanent validation or authorization failures require a different recovery path than transient connectivity loss.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Simulate acceptance followed by a dropped response. Reconnect, replay and verify the final revision did not increase again. Inject permanent denial and check that blind retries stop.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Use a deterministic clock to measure backoff bounds and cancellation, including restart. Record pending and terminal outcomes and distinguish simulated responses from real network fault tests.
