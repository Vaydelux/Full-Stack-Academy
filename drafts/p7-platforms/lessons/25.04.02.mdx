# Adaptive streaming concepts

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A video player experiences changing bandwidth.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Adaptive streaming selects renditions from a manifest based on measured bandwidth and buffer; packaging and codecs matter.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Generate synthetic local media renditions with an authorized sample; inspect manifest and test switching in a real player.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Playback remains coherent when bandwidth changes.

**Negative:** Invalid segments or unsupported codec fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Adaptive streaming packages media into renditions with different bitrates or resolutions and describes them in a manifest. The player chooses based on bandwidth, buffer and decoding capability. Segment duration and keyframe alignment affect switching, seek behavior and request overhead.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Generate renditions from a short self-created clip in an isolated environment. Inspect the manifest and play under two controlled bandwidth profiles; check startup, switching and seek behavior rather than just successful packaging.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Record codecs, rendition sizes, segment durations and player observations. Include an unsupported codec or missing segment and document recovery. Do not claim browser or device compatibility without testing it.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual media tools, storage, playback, provider and reviewer evidence.
