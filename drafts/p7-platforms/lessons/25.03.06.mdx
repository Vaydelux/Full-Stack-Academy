# Geofence queries

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

An app evaluates entry into a geographic zone.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Geofence semantics need boundary rules, accuracy uncertainty, coordinate system and authorization.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

In disposable PostGIS define a synthetic polygon, test inside/outside/boundary and accuracy tolerance.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Chosen boundary rule is explicit and repeatable.

**Negative:** Treating uncertain position as exact or bypassing permission fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Geofence inclusion depends on the boundary predicate and coordinate accuracy. A point on a polygon boundary may be included by one predicate and excluded by another. Define business behavior for uncertain location and distinguish geographic eligibility from authorization to perform an action.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Build a synthetic polygon and test interior, exterior and boundary points using the chosen PostGIS predicates. Add a location with uncertainty that overlaps the boundary and record the business decision.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Specify the accuracy threshold, coordinate system, update freshness and permission checks. Create a review matrix for uncertain, stale and denied locations; do not use a geofence as the only authorization mechanism.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual PostGIS, browser, device and reviewer evidence.
