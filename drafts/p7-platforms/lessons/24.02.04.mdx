# Partial sync and cursors

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A device downloads a partial change feed.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Cursors must belong to a defined stable ordering and scope; checkpoint only after applying a complete page.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Design an ordered synthetic feed with page size two and a crash before cursor commit.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Replay does not skip or duplicate applied changes.

**Negative:** Advancing cursor before data persistence loses records.

## Qualification boundary

In-memory sync model; actual SQLite persistence, durable queue, crash recovery and conflict reconciliation are not established.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A synchronization cursor belongs to a specific feed, ordering and authorized scope. Use stable ordering with a tie breaker or a server sequence. Persist the cursor in the same local transaction as the complete page so partial application cannot skip records.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Create five records with tied timestamps, use pages of two, and interrupt midway through the second page. Restart from the prior checkpoint and verify visible records are not duplicated or skipped.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Exercise a wrong-account cursor and an expired cursor. Design bounded resnapshot and record the cursor, applied record IDs and database commit state.
