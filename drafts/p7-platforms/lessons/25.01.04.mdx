# Warehouse concepts

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A company integrates several source systems into reporting.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Warehouse layers separate ingestion, cleaning and consumption; define grain, lineage and late arrivals.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Sketch raw, staging and curated tables for synthetic orders; define unique source IDs.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** One curated order has traceable source and timestamp.

**Negative:** Joining different grains and double-counting revenue fails.

## Qualification boundary

In-memory count/deduplication model; no warehouse, materialized-view freshness, ClickHouse or dbt runtime qualification.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A warehouse pipeline separates raw ingestion, standardized staging and curated business metrics. Each layer needs documented ownership, grain, lineage and incremental processing rules. Immutable raw data still needs a reviewed retention policy; do not assume indefinite storage is acceptable.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Create synthetic raw order events containing duplicates, late arrivals and malformed amounts. Define a staging transformation and show how curated totals are derived without counting the same order twice.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Draw a lineage graph and identify stable source keys, rejected-row handling and a rebuild path. Compare a full rebuild to incremental replay against the same fixed expected totals.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual database, analytics tools and reviewer evidence.
