# Content moderation and abuse controls

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A media service receives abusive or unsafe uploads.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Quarantine, review policy, rate limits, appeals and audit trails are distinct from automated classification.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Model synthetic moderation states pending, approved, blocked with explicit reviewer access and error handling.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Only approved allowed media is published.

**Negative:** Scanner failure treated as approval or unreviewed public upload fails.

Inspect the complementary `mediaAllowed` teaching model. Extend its assertions when relevant; it is not an implementation of the full external technology.

## Qualification boundary

Synthetic resource-bound model; no actual FFmpeg sandbox, CDN, HLS, WebRTC or moderation service acceptance.

## Knowledge check

1. What is the invariant and trust boundary?
2. Which negative result falsifies an unsafe design?
3. Which real tool or reviewer must supply missing evidence?

**Assessment:** Record exact fixture input, expected and actual output, negative evidence and not-run checks. Do not substitute a design or model for a real acceptance result.
