# Signed media URLs

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A private media URL is forwarded to another user.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Signed URLs are scoped bearer capabilities with short expiry; authorization must precede issuance and cache policy must respect it.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Design synthetic object ID, expiry and audience policy; test expired and wrong-object capability in a real local signer.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Valid scoped capability retrieves intended object.

**Negative:** Expired or altered capability fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A signed URL is a time-limited bearer capability for a particular resource and operation. Authorization must occur before issuance; an unexpired link may remain usable by anyone possessing it. Keep scope narrow, avoid logging capability strings and ensure CDN policies do not expose private representations.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Generate test capabilities with a disposable local signer for one synthetic object. Compare valid, expired and wrong-object requests. Verify the issuer denies a foreign account before producing a signature.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Specify expiry, operation, object identity, issuance audit and revocation limitations. Record sanitized verification outcomes, including caching behavior; do not expose actual credentials or signed production URLs.
