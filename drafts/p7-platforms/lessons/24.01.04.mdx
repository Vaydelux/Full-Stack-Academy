# Android lifecycle

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify native platforms, real providers or production deployments.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

An Android app is backgrounded during an edit.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Lifecycle transitions may interrupt asynchronous work; persist intent before relying on in-memory state.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

In a disposable app edit a task, background and terminate it; relaunch and reconcile the queue.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** The persisted edit survives and resumes once.

**Negative:** Lost or duplicate work after process death fails.

## Qualification boundary

Native builds, WebView isolation and plugin permissions require a real device.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

An app can lose its process after entering the background; a lifecycle callback is not guaranteed to run before every termination. Persist a small validated edit record before reporting it as durable. Use a stable operation identity when reconciling uncertain remote writes, and distinguish a queued operation from one acknowledged by the server.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Create a synthetic task edit, interrupt the application before acknowledgement, and relaunch. Compare persisted intent with the visible pending state. A duplicate server effect after replay is a different defect from a lost local edit and needs its own assertion.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Exercise process death before persistence, after persistence and after remote acknowledgement. Record expected recovery for each point, then use an authorized device to observe actual outcomes. Do not claim exactly-once delivery from a local queue.
