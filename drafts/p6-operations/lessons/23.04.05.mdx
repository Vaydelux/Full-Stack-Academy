# Data partitioning

> Unpublished instructor manuscript. Local model tests are supplemental, not external runtime qualification.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the relevant engineering tradeoff, define a measurable experiment and recognize the boundary of the available evidence.

## Context

A large dataset is split across partitions.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Partition key affects balance, query locality and ordering; hot keys and cross-partition invariants need attention.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Assign synthetic tenant workloads to partitions; introduce a hot tenant and compare load distribution.

Use synthetic data and a disposable authorized environment. Record the expected result before the experiment.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Partitioning distributes data or workload according to a chosen key. Hashing can distribute many keys while still concentrating a large tenant on one partition. Cross-partition queries and transactions may become more expensive. Choose the partition key according to actual access and ownership patterns.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Assign synthetic tenant workloads to four partitions and introduce one disproportionately busy tenant. Compare the distribution before and after a proposed key change. Check whether the change preserves required tenant isolation and query behavior.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Design a partition strategy with skew monitoring, rebalancing and migration constraints. Record modeled distribution and measured workload evidence separately.
