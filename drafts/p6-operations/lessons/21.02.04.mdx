# Circuit breakers

> Unpublished instructor manuscript. Local model tests are supplemental, not external runtime qualification.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the relevant engineering tradeoff, define a measurable experiment and recognize the boundary of the available evidence.

## Context

A remote dependency repeatedly times out.

## Step 1 — Mental model

A circuit breaker opens after a failure rule, rejects calls during cooldown and probes recovery in half-open state.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Design a fake-clock breaker with three failures and one half-open probe; record transitions.

Use synthetic data and a disposable authorized environment. Record the expected result before the experiment.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Open state short-circuits calls; successful probe closes.

**Negative:** Unbounded probes or immediate retry storms fail.

## Qualification boundary

Synthetic error-budget arithmetic only; real probes, alerts, concurrency and fault recovery require actual isolated services.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A circuit breaker limits repeated calls to a failing dependency. Closed permits calls, open rejects them quickly, and half-open admits a bounded probe. Thresholds and cooldown depend on workload; a breaker does not make ambiguous external writes safe to retry.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Use a fake clock and a scripted provider with three failures. Verify the open state makes no additional provider calls, then advance the clock and allow one probe. Test both successful and failed probes.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Design a breaker with bounded probes, failure classification and observability. Record state transitions and call counts; actual transport failure behavior requires integration testing.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve the existing specification, model and unpublished status. Actual resilience and runtime qualification remain independent.
