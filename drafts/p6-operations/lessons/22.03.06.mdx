# Cost-aware scaling

> Unpublished instructor manuscript. Local model tests are supplemental, not external runtime qualification.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the relevant engineering tradeoff, define a measurable experiment and recognize the boundary of the available evidence.

## Context

A small team must scale under a strict budget.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Use measured workload and marginal cost, caps and service limits; avoid autoscaling without spend controls.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Compare two synthetic scenarios by required instances and assumed per-instance cost; record assumptions.

Use synthetic data and a disposable authorized environment. Record the expected result before the experiment.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Plan meets budget and explicitly names unresolved bottlenecks.

**Negative:** Ignoring fixed DB or egress cost invalidates comparison.

## Qualification boundary

Capacity arithmetic only; no actual CDN, cache cluster, balancing, cost quotes or scaling benchmark.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Scaling decisions need a cost model across compute, database, storage, network transfer and operational time. A lower per-instance price may not produce a lower total cost when additional replicas or dependencies are required. Use explicit assumed prices rather than pretending to know current provider charges.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Compare two synthetic configurations with specified instance counts and hypothetical unit costs. Include fixed database and transfer costs, then vary peak demand. Inspect whether each plan remains within its stated cap and latency objective.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Write a decision record with cost assumptions, resource constraints and a measurable upgrade trigger. Replace assumptions with current official pricing and measured usage only during actual deployment planning.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded capacity or benchmark results. Preserve the existing specification, model and unpublished status. Actual deployment measurements remain independent.

## Knowledge check

1. Which invariant or operational measurement determines success?
2. What failure case disproves the unsafe design?
3. Which evidence remains missing before this can be applied to a production service?

**Assessment:** Submit input, expected/observed outcomes, a meaningful negative result and the unverified external boundaries. Mark unavailable tests not-run.
