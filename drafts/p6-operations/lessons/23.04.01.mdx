# CAP tradeoffs and practical consistency

> Unpublished instructor manuscript. Local model tests are supplemental, not external runtime qualification.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the relevant engineering tradeoff, define a measurable experiment and recognize the boundary of the available evidence.

## Context

Two replicas cannot communicate during a write.

## Step 1 — Mental model

CAP describes consistency and availability during partitions, not a fixed pick-two menu under normal operation.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Sketch a partition between replicas; choose reject versus accept conflicting writes for inventory and explain business impact.

Use synthetic data and a disposable authorized environment. Record the expected result before the experiment.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Choice preserves the explicitly selected invariant under partition.

**Negative:** Claiming both linearizable writes and unconditional availability under partition fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

CAP concerns consistency and availability during a network partition, under precise definitions of those guarantees. For inventory, rejecting a write that cannot reach the authoritative state may preserve an invariant at the cost of availability. Allowing conflicting writes requires a defined reconciliation contract, not a promise of linearizability.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Draw two replicas separated by a synthetic partition and submit competing requests for the last item. Compare refusing an unavailable authoritative write with accepting both requests for later reconciliation. Identify which outcome preserves the selected business invariant.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Write a decision record for inventory writes and a less critical read projection. Record partition assumptions, permitted responses and recovery criteria; do not claim a simulated decision proves a distributed implementation.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve the existing specification, model and unpublished status. Actual distributed runtime qualification remains independent.
