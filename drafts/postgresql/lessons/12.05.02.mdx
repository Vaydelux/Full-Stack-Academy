## Operational problem

**Replication and replicas.** Streaming replicas may lag or disappear. Production read routing needs explicit consistency requirements, role monitoring, and a failover strategy.

**Common failure:** The starter checks whether the local server is in recovery but does not inspect sender/receiver status or whether a replica is actually caught up.

Database operations differ from syntax tutorials: observing a configuration setting, successfully creating a backup file, and actually recovering an application are three distinct kinds of evidence. A successful command cannot prove availability, restoration, consistency, or acceptable recovery time without a controlled independent drill.

## Applied SQL walkthrough

Physical streaming replication sends WAL from a primary to a standby. A standby may replay changes later than they commit on the primary, so read-after-write on a replica can be stale. The sender statistics are observed on the primary, receiver statistics on the standby; empty results may reflect topology or privileges. Replication is not a backup because erroneous writes may also replicate.

### Predict and diagnose the result

Use two dedicated, correctly configured test instances. Identify each role with pg_is_in_recovery, then observe sender and receiver status and WAL positions. Commit a synthetic marker on the primary and measure when it becomes visible on the standby. Lag columns can be NULL and do not always represent a forecast of catch-up time.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Design routing for an application that needs immediate confirmation after a write but can tolerate stale reporting reads. Explain when to use the primary, what happens during a standby outage, and how to avoid split-brain during failover. Record role, marker visibility and observed lag; do not promote a real replica in this exercise.

## Practice extension

Write an operational runbook that can be followed by a second developer who has never seen your environment. Specify the real host selector, approved test database name, least-privilege role, safety checks, expected output, cleanup, and an audit of what did or did not succeed.

For backup/restore tasks, attach only sanitized non-sensitive evidence. Explain the distinction between a backup, a restore drill, and point-in-time recovery; none of those should be marked qualified from a source-only check. For permission work, test a denied operation as deliberately as a successful read.
