# Abuse management

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A platform encounters automated abuse.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Abuse management combines bounded rate controls, review, appeals, and scoped enforcement.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Design synthetic abuse cases with temporary restriction, reviewer decision and false-positive appeal.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Restrictions have reason and expiry or review path.

**Negative:** Silent permanent ban or broad tenant-wide penalty without policy fails.

## Qualification boundary

Synthetic tenant-admin authorization model only; actual RBAC, audit integrity, support workflows and migration safety require runtime acceptance.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Abuse management combines bounded preventive controls with accountable review, reversible enforcement where appropriate and an appeal process. A noisy detector does not prove wrongdoing, so separate automated signals from the final policy decision. Scope restrictions to the necessary resource, account and duration.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Use synthetic abuse signals to trigger a temporary restriction and a reviewer decision. Add a false-positive case and an expired restriction, verifying that the appropriate recovery and appeal paths remain available.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Define a state machine with reasons, reviewer authority, expiry and audit. Record decision evidence and denial cases without collecting real harmful material; test concurrent reviewer actions and reversal.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual API, database, authorization, browser and reviewer evidence.
