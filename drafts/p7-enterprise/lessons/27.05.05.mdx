## Context

A SaaS prototype needs an operating-cost forecast.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Model workload, storage, transfer, compute, third-party usage and uncertainty; obtain current prices before real commitment.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Cost planning begins with a workload model: users, requests, stored records, transferred bytes, background work and external service calls. Free-tier eligibility and billing units vary by provider and can change. Separate fixed costs from usage-driven costs, and make uncertainty and hard resource limits visible.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Build low, base and high hypothetical monthly scenarios using consistent units. Double one variable at a time and identify the largest cost driver. Check that a hypothetical increase in retries or media delivery is represented rather than hidden in an average.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Prepare an assumption table with usage quantities, unit-price source and verification date before using real prices. Use clearly labeled hypothetical rates for practice and define budget alerts, quotas and a stop condition without provisioning paid resources.
