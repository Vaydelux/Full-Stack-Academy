# Redis cache invalidation

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A task update makes cached list data stale.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Invalidate or version derived caches after committed writes; keys must include appropriate tenant scope.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Use disposable Redis and synthetic tasks; warm cache, update, invalidate and test another tenant.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Updated own list converges; foreign cache isolated.

**Negative:** Stale data permanently retained or cross-tenant leakage fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Cache entries are derived state, not the authoritative task record. Include tenant and relevant authorization scope in keys, bound lifetime and define invalidation or versioning after database commit. An old asynchronous cache fill can otherwise overwrite newer results after invalidation.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Warm a disposable Redis cache for two tenants, commit a task update and invalidate or advance its version. Delay an older read until after the update and verify the stale result cannot be installed as current. Check that an unrelated tenant remains unaffected.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Record key structure, TTL, hit and miss behavior, authoritative version and recovery when Redis is unavailable. Compare correctness before and after caching, rather than treating a cache hit as proof of authorized or fresh data.
