# Fault injection drill

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A dependency fails during a technical defense.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Bounded fault drills demonstrate behavior, fallback, recovery and observability in an isolated environment.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Inject one synthetic dependency timeout and record impact, abort criteria and restoration.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** System follows documented failure behavior and recovers.

**Negative:** Unbounded retries, missing recovery or live-service disruption fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A fault drill tests a specific resilience hypothesis with bounded impact, observable signals, an abort condition and a restoration plan. Choose an isolated dependency and predict what the user and authoritative state should experience. An uncontrolled outage is not a useful substitute for a designed experiment.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Introduce a synthetic dependency timeout in the disposable workspace. Observe request latency, bounded retries, fallback behavior and persisted state; stop if the agreed limit is exceeded. Restore the dependency and verify the user journey and queued work recover.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Record the experiment setup, actual fault duration, signals, user-visible impact, recovery and any unexpected effect. Compare results to the hypothesis and identify the next corrective test. Keep production systems outside the exercise.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual implementation, review, recovery, cost and candidate evidence.
