# Session and device controls

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

An organization loses an employee laptop.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Sessions and devices need revocation, expiry, refresh controls and server-side enforcement.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Use synthetic sessions for two devices; revoke one and exercise subsequent protected requests.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Revoked session denied; unaffected session follows policy.

**Negative:** UI-only revocation that leaves tokens active fails.

## Qualification boundary

Synthetic policy check on already-validated claims only; no actual SAML assertion, SCIM, MFA, token or identity-provider verification.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

A device listing describes sessions or installation associations; it is not itself a revocation mechanism. Enforce expiry, token verification and server-side revocation according to the chosen session architecture. Short-lived access tokens may remain valid until expiration unless additional checks invalidate them.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Create two synthetic device sessions, revoke one and attempt the same protected action using each. Inspect whether access and refresh paths enforce the intended revocation policy, including a request already in flight.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Define session identifiers, expiry, refresh rotation, logout and compromised-device recovery. Record access outcomes and any unavoidable revocation delay; keep credentials out of test evidence.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual IdP, application authorization, database and reviewer evidence.
