# Grace periods and dunning

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A customer's renewal fails temporarily.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Grace periods and dunning require explicit policy, paid-through date and customer communications.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Run entitlement for past_due before and at paidUntil; define notification schedule.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Access follows documented grace cutoff.

**Negative:** Unbounded grace or immediate denial contrary to policy fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Grace and dunning are explicit policy decisions, not incidental consequences of callback timing. Define paid-through time, notification stages, retry rules and the access cutoff. A failed renewal does not necessarily imply immediate loss of access, and an indefinite grace period can violate the business policy.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Use a fixed synthetic clock to evaluate past-due state just before, exactly at and after the paidUntil boundary. Compare successful retry and unrecovered failure, and inspect the resulting access decision.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Build a notification and recovery timeline with deterministic dates and idempotent message identities. Record cutoff semantics and the user-facing state; verify actual provider retry behavior separately.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status; independently verify real sandbox signatures, database durability, billing and reviewer evidence.
