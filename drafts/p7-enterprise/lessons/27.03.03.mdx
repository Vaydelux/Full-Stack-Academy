# Notification queue

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A task assignment sends a notification.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Durable queue and outbox separate transaction from external delivery; retries need stable identifiers.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Implement a disposable assignment/outbox transaction and fake sender with duplicate and failed delivery.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** One logical notification is tracked with retry outcome.

**Negative:** Email sent for rolled-back assignment or duplicate effect fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

An assignment and its notification intent should commit together through a durable outbox; external delivery happens independently. A worker needs a stable job identity, bounded retries and a way to distinguish accepted work from an uncertain acknowledgement. A failed mail request must not roll back an already committed assignment.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Write an assignment and outbox row in one disposable transaction, then simulate sender failure and acknowledgement loss. Retry the same job identity and inspect the number of logical effects. Force the database transaction to fail and verify no orphan notification remains.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Prepare a delivery state and retry matrix including permanent rejection, worker restart and duplicate delivery. Record persisted intent and delivery attempts separately; do not infer exactly-once external delivery from a fake sender.
