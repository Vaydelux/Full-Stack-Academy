# Data residency

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A vendor stores backups in a different region.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Data residency maps where processing and copies occur; cross-border transfer obligations require separate legal assessment.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Draw data flow for app, backups, telemetry and support; identify unknown locations and contract questions.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Every processing location and owner is known or flagged.

**Negative:** Assuming a database region guarantees all processing stays there fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Data residency concerns where data is processed and where copies exist, including backups, telemetry, content delivery and support access. Selecting a database region does not establish that every processing activity stays in that region. Transfer obligations depend on the reviewed factual and legal context.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Draw a synthetic application flow from browser to API, database, backup, telemetry and support. Mark every known region and identify unknown routing or processor locations rather than assuming a single region.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Prepare a location and processor inventory with verification source, contract questions and owners. Record gaps for qualified review; do not claim a deployment is legally compliant based on its region setting.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status; actual controls, legal applicability, runtime and independent review remain separate gates.
