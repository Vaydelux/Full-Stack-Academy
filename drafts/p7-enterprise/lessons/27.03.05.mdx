# Live updates

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A teammate receives task changes in real time.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Live updates need authorization, scoped subscriptions, versioning and reconnect reconciliation.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Use local websocket/SSE fixture with two tenants; drop connection and replay missed updates.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Authorized updates arrive and reconnect converges.

**Negative:** Foreign event or stale state after reconnect fails.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Realtime messages supplement the authoritative workspace state; they do not replace authorized reads and writes. Scope subscriptions to verified membership, include event identity or revision and reconcile gaps after reconnect. A client that has lost access must not continue receiving protected updates.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Connect two synthetic tenants through a disposable SSE or WebSocket fixture. Send an authorized update, attempt a foreign subscription, interrupt connectivity and reconnect with a known checkpoint. Remove membership and verify the documented revocation behavior.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Create an event timeline with duplicate, delayed and missing updates. Record observed order, reconciliation requests and final visible state. Keep transport-level delivery claims distinct from actual database consistency and user authorization.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual API, storage, database, queue, cache, browser and reviewer evidence.
