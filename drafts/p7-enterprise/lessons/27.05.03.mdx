# Architecture tradeoff presentation

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

A learner explains why the system is modular rather than distributed.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Architecture defense compares alternatives using constraints, measurements, failure domains and migration costs.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Present ADR and diagram; explain when service extraction would become justified.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Decision includes tradeoffs and measurable trigger.

**Negative:** Tool names alone or unsupported scale claims fail.

## Qualification boundary

Final defense rubric only; actual human review, exploit verification, performance, costs and upgrade rehearsal remain separate.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

Architecture defense explains why the selected design fits measured constraints and what would justify changing it. Compare alternatives using coupling, ownership, latency, failure domains, cost and migration effort. A diagram of many services is not evidence of scalability or good boundaries.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Present the task workspace ADR and trace one user request through its data and trust boundaries. Compare the current design with a separate service under a hypothetical scaling or ownership change, and identify the new failure modes introduced by extraction.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Define measurable reversal criteria, a safe migration path and the contracts that must remain stable. Support claims with actual tests or measurements where available and explicitly identify assumptions; do not invent workload evidence to defend the design.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual implementation, review, recovery, cost and candidate evidence.

## Knowledge check

1. What is the invariant and trust boundary?
2. Which negative result falsifies an unsafe design?
3. Which real tool or reviewer must supply missing evidence?

**Assessment:** Record exact fixture input, expected and actual output, negative evidence and not-run checks. Do not substitute a design or model for a real acceptance result.
