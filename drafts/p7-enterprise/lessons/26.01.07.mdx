# Auditability

> Instructor draft. Local models do not qualify external identity, billing, privacy compliance or an actual capstone release.

## Learning outcomes

Explain the engineering decision, reproduce or design the positive and negative experiment, and identify the remaining evidence.

## Context

An auditor investigates an administrative login.

## Step 1 — Mental model

Audit records capture actor, action, target, outcome, time and correlation; secrets and raw assertions stay out.

## Step 2 — Guided practice

Generate synthetic successful and rejected events; verify minimal fields and access restrictions.

Use synthetic data, a disposable environment and authorized accounts only. Record expected behavior before execution.

## Step 3 — Acceptance

**Positive:** Both outcomes can be reconstructed without credentials.

**Negative:** Missing denied event or sensitive token in audit fails.

Inspect the complementary `enterpriseAccess` teaching model. Extend its assertions when relevant; it is not an implementation of the full external technology.

## Qualification boundary

Synthetic policy check on already-validated claims only; no actual SAML assertion, SCIM, MFA, token or identity-provider verification.

## Applied engineering walkthrough

An audit event records an attributable action, target, outcome, time and correlation identifier. Separate security audit records from verbose application logs, restrict who may read them and avoid copying secrets or assertions. Integrity and retention require explicit storage controls beyond a well-shaped JSON object.

### Diagnose the concrete failure

Generate synthetic successful and rejected administrative actions. Verify required fields, correct tenant scope and absence of tokens, raw assertions and unnecessary personal data. Try reading another tenant's event.

### Independent exercise and evidence

Design access rules, retention and tamper-evidence requirements. Record sanitized events and negative query evidence, including a failed write to the audit sink and the chosen fail-open or fail-closed policy.

**Evidence boundary:** These are practice instructions, not recorded results. Preserve draft status and independently verify actual IdP, application authorization, database and reviewer evidence.

## Knowledge check

1. What is the invariant and trust boundary?
2. Which negative result falsifies an unsafe design?
3. Which real tool or reviewer must supply missing evidence?

**Assessment:** Record exact fixture input, expected and actual output, negative evidence and not-run checks. Do not substitute a design or model for a real acceptance result.
