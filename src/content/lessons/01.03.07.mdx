## The problem we're solving

An attendance chart has alt text 'chart' and no accessible numbers, so learners cannot determine the difference between Monday and Tuesday sessions. Decorative flourishes repeat the page title in the screen-reader reading order.

## Build the mental model

Alternative text communicates an image's purpose or relevant information in context. Informative diagrams need meaningful alt text; purely decorative images should usually have alt="" so they do not add noise. Complex charts may require a nearby table or detailed textual summary to communicate all values, not a huge alt paragraph. A figure caption gives context to visual and non-visual readers. SVG can be embedded as a normal img with alt supplied by HTML. A screen-reader image list should include the meaningful diagram while omitting decoration.

**Predict before running:** A chart of 12 attendees on Monday and 18 on Tuesday cannot be understood from alt='chart'. Describe the trend and provide an actual data table.

<LessonStep number={3} title={"Pair a complex chart with accessible numbers"} file="labs/a11y-image-alt/starter/index.html" expected={"The table gives accessible exact data alongside the visual trend."}>

Use a concise image alternative and adjacent table; let readers explore exact values.

```html
<figure><img src="assets/attendance.svg" width="640" height="320" alt="Workshop attendance increased from 12 Monday to 18 Tuesday">
<figcaption>Attendance during two practice sessions</figcaption></figure>
<table><caption>Workshop attendance by day</caption>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Day</th><th scope="col">Attendees</th></tr></thead>
<tbody><tr><th scope="row">Monday</th><td>12</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Tuesday</th><td>18</td></tr></tbody></table>
```

</LessonStep>
